=== fire | Fire | 🔥 | #D2491E | Fire safety, building, lighting and putting out

# fire-safety | Fire Safety | The Firem'n Chit rules
Scouts earn the Firem'n Chit by showing they understand fire safety. Its rules are good for everyone.
- Get permission and check fire regulations before building any fire.
- Use an existing fire ring whenever possible.
- Clear a 10-foot circle of anything that can burn: leaves, grass, branches.
- Keep the fire small. Never build it under branches or near tents.
- Keep a bucket of water and a shovel ready.
- Never leave a fire unattended, not even for a minute.
- Never use liquid fuel, gasoline, or aerosol on a fire.
- Do not play with fire or burning sticks.
! In dry or windy weather, there may be fire bans. Respect them.

# fire-building | Building a Fire | Tinder, kindling, and fuel
A fire needs three things: heat, fuel, and air. Take away any one and the fire dies.
## Materials
- Tinder: fine, dry material that catches a spark or match. Dry grass, birch bark, pine needles, wood shavings, dryer lint, cotton balls with petroleum jelly.
- Kindling: dry twigs from pencil-lead to thumb thickness.
- Fuel wood: dead and down wood, wrist thickness or smaller. Never cut live trees.
Gather all three before you light. Gather twice as much as you think you need.
## Fire lays
- Teepee: lean kindling around the tinder like a cone. Lights fast and burns hot, good for cooking quickly.
- Log cabin: build a square of sticks around a teepee. Burns steady, good for cooking coals.
- Lean-to: lean kindling against a larger stick over the tinder. Good in wind.
- Star: push long logs into the center as they burn. Saves wood.

> Wood should snap when you break it. If it bends, it is too green or damp.

# fire-starting | Lighting a Fire | Matches, lighters, and ferro rods
1. Kneel with your back to the wind.
2. Strike the match away from you and hold it so the flame climbs the stick.
3. Light the tinder on the windward side so the wind pushes flame into the fuel.
4. Add kindling gradually. Blow gently at the base if it needs air.
5. Add fuel wood as the kindling burns well.
## Ferro rod (fire steel)
Scrape the striker down the rod firmly, aiming sparks into a nest of very fine, dry tinder. Hold the striker still and pull the rod back to avoid scattering your tinder.
> Carry matches in a waterproof container with a striker, plus a lighter as a backup.

# fire-out | Putting Out a Fire | Drown, stir, and feel
1. Let the fire burn down to ash if you have time.
2. Drown it: pour water slowly over all the embers.
3. Stir it: mix the ashes and embers with a stick or shovel.
4. Drown it again.
5. Feel it: hold the back of your hand near the ashes. If you feel any heat, repeat.

! It is not out until it is cold out. Never bury a fire with dirt only; it can smolder for days.
