# rope-care | Rope Care | Whipping and fusing ends
The end of a rope is the working end. The long unused part is the standing part. A loop made by folding the rope back on itself is a bight.
## Whipping a rope end
1. Make a loop of thin twine along the rope with the loop pointing toward the end.
@ knots/rope-care-1.xml | Step 1: Make a loop of thin twine along the rope with the loop pointing toward the end.
2. Wrap the twine tightly around the rope and the loop, working toward the end, about as wide as the rope is thick.
@ knots/rope-care-2.xml | Step 2: Wrap the twine tightly around the rope and the loop, working toward the end, about as wide as the rope is thick.
3. Slip the end of the twine through the loop.
@ knots/rope-care-3.xml | Step 3: Slip the end of the twine through the loop.
4. Pull the other end of the twine to draw the loop under the wraps. Trim both ends.
@ knots/rope-care-4.xml | Step 4: Pull the other end of the twine to draw the loop under the wraps. Trim both ends.
## Fusing
Synthetic rope can be fused by carefully melting the end with a flame. Do it outside with adult supervision. The melted plastic is very hot; do not touch it.
> Keep rope dry, coil it neatly, and do not step on it.

# square-knot | Square Knot | Bind bandages and bundles
Use it to tie bandages, bundles, and two ends of the same rope.
1. Take one end in each hand.
@ knots/square-knot-1.xml | Step 1: Take one end in each hand.
2. Pass the right end over the left and under. "Right over left."
@ knots/square-knot-2.xml | Step 2: Pass the right end over the left and under. "Right over left."
3. Bring the ends back up. Pass the left end over the right and under. "Left over right."
@ knots/square-knot-3.xml | Step 3: Bring the ends back up. Pass the left end over the right and under. "Left over right."
4. Pull tight. Both parts of each rope lie side by side.
@ knots/square-knot-4.xml | Step 4: Pull tight. Both parts of each rope lie side by side.
! If the ends stick out at an angle, you tied a granny knot. Untie it and try again.
! Never use a square knot for climbing or to join ropes of different sizes. It can slip.

# two-half-hitches | Two Half Hitches | Tie a rope to a post
Use it to tie a rope to a tree, post, or ring, such as a clothesline.
1. Pass the end around the post.
@ knots/two-half-hitches-1.xml | Step 1: Pass the end around the post.
2. Bring it over the standing part, then around and back through the loop you just made. That is one half hitch.
@ knots/two-half-hitches-2.xml | Step 2: Bring it over the standing part, then around and back through the loop you just made. That is one half hitch.
3. Do it again in the same direction below the first.
@ knots/two-half-hitches-3.xml | Step 3: Do it again in the same direction below the first.
4. Slide the hitches snug against the post.
@ knots/two-half-hitches-4.xml | Step 4: Slide the hitches snug against the post.

# taut-line | Taut-Line Hitch | An adjustable loop
Use it for tent guylines because it slides to tighten and holds under tension.
1. Pass the rope around a stake or tree.
@ knots/taut-line-1.xml | Step 1: Pass the rope around a stake or tree.
2. Bring the end around the standing part and through the loop, making two wraps inside the loop toward the stake.
@ knots/taut-line-2.xml | Step 2: Bring the end around the standing part and through the loop, making two wraps inside the loop toward the stake.
3. Bring the end around the standing part once more, outside the loop, and through.
@ knots/taut-line-3.xml | Step 3: Bring the end around the standing part once more, outside the loop, and through.
4. Tighten. Slide the knot up to tighten the line, down to loosen.
@ knots/taut-line-4.xml | Step 4: Tighten. Slide the knot up to tighten the line, down to loosen.

# bowline | Bowline | A fixed loop for camp tasks
A fixed loop for ordinary camp tasks. The loop does not tighten under steady load, but a bowline can loosen when unloaded.
1. Make a small overhand loop in the standing part. This is the rabbit hole.
@ knots/bowline-1.xml | Step 1: Make a small overhand loop in the standing part. This is the rabbit hole.
2. The rabbit (working end) comes up through the hole.
@ knots/bowline-2.xml | Step 2: The rabbit (working end) comes up through the hole.
3. Goes around the back of the tree (the standing part).
@ knots/bowline-3.xml | Step 3: Goes around the back of the tree (the standing part).
4. And back down the hole.
@ knots/bowline-4.xml | Step 4: And back down the hole.
5. Hold the rabbit and loop together and pull the standing part to tighten.
@ knots/bowline-5.xml | Step 5: Hold the rabbit and loop together and pull the standing part to tighten.
> Practice with spare rope until you can identify the nipping loop, collar, and tail.
! These diagrams are for camp practice. Climbing and rescue require trained supervision, approved equipment, and inspected knots.

# clove-hitch | Clove Hitch | Start and finish lashings
1. Wrap the rope around the pole.
@ knots/clove-hitch-1.xml | Step 1: Wrap the rope around the pole.
2. Cross over the first wrap and go around the pole again.
@ knots/clove-hitch-2.xml | Step 2: Cross over the first wrap and go around the pole again.
3. Tuck the end under the second wrap so it comes out between the wraps, going the opposite direction from the standing part.

# timber-hitch | Timber Hitch | Drag a log or start a diagonal lashing
1. Pass the rope around the log.
@ knots/timber-hitch-1.xml | Step 1: Pass the rope around the log.
2. Wrap the end around the standing part once.
@ knots/timber-hitch-2.xml | Step 2: Wrap the end around the standing part once.
3. Twist the end around itself three or more times.
@ knots/timber-hitch-3.xml | Step 3: Twist the end around itself three or more times.
4. Pull the standing part so the hitch grips the log. It comes loose easily when the pull stops.
@ knots/timber-hitch-4.xml | Step 4: Pull the standing part so the hitch grips the log. It comes loose easily when the pull stops.

# sheet-bend | Sheet Bend | Join ropes of different sizes
1. Make a bight in the end of the thicker rope.
@ knots/sheet-bend-1.xml | Step 1: Make a bight in the end of the thicker rope.
2. Pass the end of the thinner rope up through the bight.
@ knots/sheet-bend-2.xml | Step 2: Pass the end of the thinner rope up through the bight.
3. Wrap it around behind both parts of the bight.
@ knots/sheet-bend-3.xml | Step 3: Wrap it around behind both parts of the bight.
4. Tuck it under itself, not under the bight.
@ knots/sheet-bend-4.xml | Step 4: Tuck it under itself, not under the bight.
5. Pull tight.

# square-lashing | Square Lashing | Bind poles at a right angle
1. Tie a clove hitch on the upright pole just below where the crosspiece will sit.
@ knots/square-lashing-1.xml | Step 1: Tie a clove hitch on the upright pole just below where the crosspiece will sit.
2. Wrap the rope over the crosspiece, behind the upright, under the crosspiece, and behind the upright again. Make 3 or 4 wraps.
@ knots/square-lashing-2.xml | Step 2: Wrap the rope over the crosspiece, behind the upright, under the crosspiece, and behind the upright again. Make 3 or 4 wraps.
3. Make 2 or 3 frapping turns: wrap between the poles, around the wraps, pulling tight.
@ knots/square-lashing-3.xml | Step 3: Make 2 or 3 frapping turns: wrap between the poles, around the wraps, pulling tight.
4. Finish with a clove hitch on the crosspiece.
@ knots/square-lashing-4.xml | Step 4: Finish with a clove hitch on the crosspiece.
> Used for building camp gadgets like tables, racks, and towers.

# diagonal-lashing | Diagonal Lashing | Spring poles together
Used when poles cross at an angle and need to be pulled together, like an X brace.
1. Tie a timber hitch around both poles where they cross.
@ knots/diagonal-lashing-1.xml | Step 1: Tie a timber hitch around both poles where they cross.
2. Make 3 wraps around the poles in the same direction as the timber hitch.
@ knots/diagonal-lashing-2.xml | Step 2: Make 3 wraps around the poles in the same direction as the timber hitch.
3. Make 3 wraps the other direction, across the first ones.
@ knots/diagonal-lashing-3.xml | Step 3: Make 3 wraps the other direction, across the first ones.
4. Add 2 frapping turns between the poles.
@ knots/diagonal-lashing-4.xml | Step 4: Add 2 frapping turns between the poles.
5. Finish with a clove hitch.
@ knots/diagonal-lashing-5.xml | Step 5: Finish with a clove hitch.

# shear-lashing | Shear Lashing | Make a two-pole A-frame
1. Lay two poles side by side. Tie a clove hitch to one pole.
@ knots/shear-lashing-1.xml | Step 1: Lay two poles side by side. Tie a clove hitch to one pole.
2. Wrap loosely around both poles 7 or 8 times.
@ knots/shear-lashing-2.xml | Step 2: Wrap loosely around both poles 7 or 8 times.
3. Make 2 frapping turns between the poles.
@ knots/shear-lashing-3.xml | Step 3: Make 2 frapping turns between the poles.
4. Finish with a clove hitch on the other pole.
@ knots/shear-lashing-4.xml | Step 4: Finish with a clove hitch on the other pole.
5. Spread the poles apart to make an A-frame. A tripod uses a separate three-pole lashing with over-and-under weaving; do not substitute this two-pole lashing.
