=== survival | Survival | 🏕️ | #4A3F35 | What to do when lost or stranded

# lost-stop | If You Are Lost: STOP | The first thing to do
The moment you realize you are lost, remember STOP.
1. Stop: stay where you are. Sit down. Do not keep wandering.
2. Think: how did you get here? What do you remember passing?
3. Observe: look around for landmarks, trails, or sounds of roads and water. Check your map and compass.
4. Plan: if you are not sure of the way, stay put and make yourself easy to find.
## Hug a tree
Searchers find people faster when they stay in one place. Pick a spot in the open near your last known location, blow your whistle, and wait.
> Fear is normal. Taking deep breaths and making a plan helps you think clearly.

# rule-of-threes | Survival Priorities | The rule of threes
A person can survive about:
- 3 minutes without air
- 3 hours without shelter in harsh weather
- 3 days without water
- 3 weeks without food
So your priorities are usually: first aid and safety, shelter, signaling, water, then food.
> Your most important survival tool is a calm, positive attitude.

# emergency-shelter | Emergency Shelter | Stay warm and dry
- Use what you have: a tarp, emergency blanket, poncho, or trash bag.
- Look for natural protection: a rock overhang, a fallen tree, or thick evergreens.
- Get off the ground. Pile dry leaves, pine needles, or your pack under you.
- Make it small. A small space is easier to keep warm.
## Debris hut
1. Prop a long, sturdy pole on a stump or forked stick, making a ramp just longer than you are tall.
2. Lean sticks along both sides to make ribs.
3. Pile leaves and debris on top, at least 2 feet thick.
4. Stuff the inside with dry leaves and crawl in feet first.

! Avoid dead trees overhead, dry streambeds, and avalanche or rockfall paths.

# signaling | Signaling for Help | Make yourself easy to find
- Whistle: three blasts, repeated. It carries farther than your voice and uses less energy.
- Three of anything is a distress signal: three fires, three piles of rocks, three flashes.
- Signal mirror: aim reflected sunlight at aircraft or distant searchers. Even a phone screen or foil works.
- Bright colors: spread out a bright jacket, tarp, or space blanket in an open area.
- Ground signals: make a large X (need medical help) or V (need help) from logs, rocks, or trampled snow. Make it big, at least 10 feet.
- Smoke: green leaves on a fire make white smoke visible in the day.
- At night, use a flashlight: three flashes.
- Phone: if you have any signal, call or text 911. A text may get through when a call cannot.

# survival-water | Finding Water | When your bottle is empty
- Look for water downhill, in valleys, and where plants are thick and green.
- Collect rain with a tarp or poncho.
- Collect morning dew by wiping grass with a cloth and wringing it out.
- Treat all water if you can. If you cannot, drinking untreated water is better than severe dehydration.
! Do not eat snow without melting it. It lowers your body temperature.

# survival-fire | Survival Fire | Warmth, signaling, and morale
- A fire warms you, dries clothing, boils water, signals rescuers, and keeps your spirits up.
- Find dry tinder under logs, inside dead standing trees, or in the lower dead branches of evergreens.
- Build a platform of sticks if the ground is wet or snowy.
- Build a reflector wall of logs or rocks behind the fire to push heat toward you.
> Practice starting a fire in the rain before you ever need to.

# survival-kit | Personal Survival Kit | A small kit that fits in a pocket
- Whistle
- Waterproof matches and a lighter, plus fire starter
- Emergency space blanket or bivy
- Small flashlight
- Signal mirror
- Water purification tablets
- Large trash bag (rain poncho, shelter, or water collector)
- Energy bar
- Small knife and length of cord
- Card with your name and emergency contact
> Carry the kit on your body, not in your pack. You might get separated from your pack.

# survival-food | Food in a Survival Situation | Lowest priority
Most people are rescued within 72 hours, long before food becomes a problem. Focus on shelter, water, and signaling first.
! Never eat wild plants, berries, or mushrooms unless you are completely sure they are safe. Many are deadly poisonous and look like safe ones.
> Ration your trail food, but do not starve yourself; your body needs fuel to stay warm.
