=== prepared | Emergency Prep | 🚨 | #9C2B23 | Calling 911, family kits and lost Scouts

# calling-911 | Calling 911 | What to say
1. Stay calm and speak clearly.
2. Say where you are first: address, trail name, campsite, landmarks, or GPS coordinates.
3. Say what happened and how many people are hurt.
4. Describe their condition: are they awake, breathing, bleeding?
5. Answer questions and follow instructions.
6. Do not hang up until the dispatcher tells you to.
> In the backcountry, write down the location and details before sending runners for help.

# lost-buddy | Lost Scout Plan | If someone goes missing
1. Stay calm. Tell an adult leader immediately.
2. Do a buddy check and headcount of everyone else.
3. Find out when and where the Scout was last seen and what they were wearing.
4. Leaders begin a quick, organized search of nearby areas. No Scout searches alone.
5. Call for help early. Search and rescue teams would rather come out for nothing than come too late.

# family-kit | Family Emergency Kit | Be prepared at home
- Water: one gallon per person per day for at least three days
- Nonperishable food for at least three days and a can opener
- Flashlights, a battery or crank radio, and extra batteries
- First aid kit and important medications
- Copies of important documents
- Phone chargers and a backup battery
- Whistle, dust masks, and plastic sheeting
- A family meeting place and a list of contacts
> Help your family make an emergency plan. It counts toward several merit badges.

# trip-plan | Trip Plan | Tell someone before you go
Before any outing, leave a written plan with someone at home:
- Where you are going and your route
- Who is going
- When you are leaving and returning
- What to do if you do not return on time
- How to contact the leader
