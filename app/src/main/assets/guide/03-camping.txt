=== camping | Camping | ⛺ | #3E6B3A | Gear, campsites, tents, and sleeping warm

# outdoor-essentials | Scout Outdoor Essentials | Carry these on every outing
These items help you stay safe and comfortable on any hike or campout.
1. Pocketknife (only if you have earned your Totin' Chip)
2. First aid kit
3. Extra clothing for the weather
4. Rain gear
5. Water bottle, full
6. Flashlight or headlamp with spare batteries
7. Trail food
8. Matches and fire starters in a waterproof container
9. Sun protection: sunscreen, hat, sunglasses
10. Map and compass, and the skill to use them
> Add a whistle. Three blasts is the signal for help.

# packing-list | Overnight Packing List | Personal gear for a campout
## Sleeping
- Sleeping bag rated for the expected low temperature
- Sleeping pad (insulates you from cold ground)
- Pillow or stuff sack filled with clothes
## Clothing
- Field uniform for travel if your troop asks for it
- Moisture-wicking shirts, hiking pants or shorts
- Warm layer such as fleece, and a warm hat
- Rain jacket
- Extra socks (wool or synthetic) and underwear
- Sturdy, broken-in footwear and camp shoes
## Eating
- Mess kit: plate, bowl, cup, spoon and fork
- Water bottle
## Hygiene
- Toothbrush, toothpaste, small towel
- Biodegradable soap, hand sanitizer
## Other
- Outdoor Essentials (see above)
- Medications in their original labeled containers
- Scout handbook, notebook, pencil
! Leave electronics, valuables, and anything not on the list at home unless your leader says otherwise.
> Pack in labeled plastic bags so everything stays dry and is easy to find.

# campsite | Choosing a Campsite | Find a safe, comfortable spot
## Look for
- Flat, durable ground that drains well, like gravel, sand, or established sites
- At least 200 feet (about 70 adult steps) from lakes and streams
- Natural wind breaks
- Morning sun to warm and dry the camp
## Avoid
- Dead trees or large dead branches above (called widowmakers)
- Low spots and dry streambeds that can flood
- Hilltops and lone trees during lightning
- Game trails and ant hills
- Fragile meadows and plants
> Use existing campsites whenever possible. Good campsites are found, not made.

# tents | Pitching a Tent | Step by step
1. Clear the spot of sticks and stones. Do not dig trenches.
2. Lay down a ground cloth, folded so it does not stick out past the tent. Water that lands on an exposed ground cloth runs under you.
3. Spread the tent out with the door facing away from the wind.
4. Stake down the corners, pulling the floor square and tight. Drive stakes at an angle, leaning away from the tent.
5. Assemble the poles and attach them to the tent body.
6. Put on the rain fly and stake out its guylines with taut-line hitches so the fly does not touch the tent.
7. Practice at home before your first campout.
## Tent rules
- No food, snacks, toothpaste, or scented items in tents. Animals will come looking.
- No flames, stoves, or heaters in or near tents.
- Take off muddy boots before going inside.

# sleeping-warm | Sleeping Warm | Stay comfortable at night
- Put a sleeping pad under your bag. The cold ground steals more heat than the air.
- Change into dry clothes before bed. Damp clothes from the day make you cold.
- Wear a warm hat to bed.
- Eat a snack before bed so your body has fuel to make heat.
- Go to the bathroom before you go to sleep.
- Put tomorrow's clothes inside your bag so they are warm in the morning.
- Fluff your bag so the insulation can trap air.
! Never use a stove, heater, or charcoal in a tent. Carbon monoxide can kill without warning.

# camp-layout | Camp Layout and Duty Roster | Organize the patrol site
## Layout
- Tents in one area, away from the kitchen
- Kitchen at least 200 feet from tents in bear country, downwind
- Fire ring away from tents and overhanging branches
- Dishwashing and gray water area away from water sources
## Duty roster
Rotate jobs so everyone learns and everyone helps. Common jobs:
- Cooks: prepare meals
- Assistant cooks: help cook, fetch water, and set up
- Cleanup crew: wash dishes and clean the kitchen
- Water and fire crew: haul water and tend the fire
> Post the roster where everyone can see it. The patrol leader makes sure the jobs get done.

# camp-hygiene | Camp Hygiene | Staying healthy in the field
- Wash your hands before cooking or eating and after using the latrine. Most camp illness comes from dirty hands.
- Keep a handwashing station near the kitchen and latrine.
- Brush your teeth and spit toothpaste away from the campsite.
- Change socks daily and keep your feet dry.
## Going to the bathroom without a toilet
1. Go at least 200 feet from water, trails, and camp.
2. Dig a cathole 6 to 8 inches deep and 4 to 6 inches wide.
3. When finished, fill the hole with the original soil and disguise it.
4. Pack out toilet paper in a sealed bag, or follow the rules of the area.

# rain-camp | Camping in the Rain | Stay dry and happy
- Pitch a dining fly or tarp over the kitchen area.
- Keep your sleeping bag and dry clothes in a waterproof bag.
- Make sure the rain fly is tight and does not touch the tent wall.
- Keep rain gear on top of your pack where you can reach it fast.
- Wet clothes stay outside the tent or in a separate bag.
> Bring a few extra plastic bags. They have a hundred uses on a rainy trip.
