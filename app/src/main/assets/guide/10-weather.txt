# lightning | Lightning Safety | When thunder roars, go indoors
- If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck.
- Get into a building or a hard-topped vehicle. Tents and picnic shelters do not protect you.
- Get off ridges, hilltops, and open fields. Get out of the water and off boats.
- Stay away from tall, lone trees, metal fences, and poles.
- Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back outside.
## If you are caught out with no shelter
1. Move to lower ground, such as a valley or a thick stand of short trees.
2. Spread the group out at least 50 feet apart so one strike does not hurt everyone.
3. Crouch on your sleeping pad or pack with feet together, head down, hands over ears.
! A person struck by lightning is safe to touch. Call 911 and start CPR if needed.

# sky-signs | Reading the Sky | Weather signs
## Fair weather signs
- Puffy white cumulus clouds with flat bottoms
- Morning dew or fog that burns off
- A red sunset ("Red sky at night, sailor's delight")
- Wind steady from the west
## Stormy weather signs
- Tall, dark cumulonimbus clouds with anvil tops
- Clouds that thicken and lower through the day
- A sudden drop in temperature or a sudden change in wind
- A ring around the moon (rain may come in a day or two)
- A red sunrise ("Red sky in morning, sailors take warning")
> Always check the forecast before a trip, and keep checking the sky.

# flash-floods | Flash Floods | Water rises fast
- Never camp in a dry wash, canyon bottom, or right next to a stream.
- Rain miles upstream can cause a flood where it is sunny.
- Watch for rising water, muddy water, floating debris, and a roaring sound.
- Climb to higher ground immediately. Do not try to outrun it down the canyon.
! Turn around, don't drown. Six inches of moving water can knock you down.

# poison-plants | Poisonous Plants | Leaves of three, let it be
## Poison ivy
Three leaflets, the middle one on a longer stem. Can be a vine, a ground cover, or a shrub. Leaves turn red in fall.
## Poison oak
Three leaflets with rounded lobes, like an oak leaf. Grows as a shrub, mostly in the West and Southeast.
## Poison sumac
A shrub or small tree with 7 to 13 leaflets on red stems. Grows in swampy areas.
## If you touch them
- Wash with soap and cool water as soon as possible.
- Wash clothing and tools.
- Never burn these plants. The smoke can cause serious lung reactions.

# wildlife | Bears and Wildlife | Respect wild animals
- Never feed or approach wildlife. Watch from a distance.
- Store food, trash, and anything with a scent (toothpaste, sunscreen) in a bear canister, bear box, or bear hang, away from tents.
- Make noise on the trail so you do not surprise animals.
## If you meet a bear
1. Stay calm. Do not run.
2. Stand tall, group together, and talk in a calm, firm voice.
3. Back away slowly, facing the bear.
4. If a black bear attacks, fight back. If a grizzly attacks, lie flat on your stomach, protect your neck with your hands, and play dead. Follow local rangers' advice.
## Mountain lions
Stand tall, make yourself look big, keep small Scouts close, and never run. If attacked, fight back.

# bugs | Bugs and Repellent | Mosquitoes and ticks
- Use an insect repellent with DEET or picaridin on skin. Clothing can be treated with permethrin (follow the label; adults apply).
- Wear long sleeves and long pants at dawn and dusk.
- Do a full tick check every evening, especially behind knees, in armpits, along the waistband, and in hair.
