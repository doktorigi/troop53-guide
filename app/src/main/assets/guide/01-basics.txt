# scout-oath | Scout Oath | The promise every Scout makes
The Scout Oath is the promise at the heart of Scouting. Learn it word for word and think about what each part means.
## The Oath
On my honor I will do my best
To do my duty to God and my country
and to obey the Scout Law;
To help other people at all times;
To keep myself physically strong,
mentally awake, and morally straight.
## What it means
- On my honor: you are putting your good name behind this promise.
- Duty to God and country: practice your faith, respect the faith of others, and be a good citizen.
- Obey the Scout Law: live by its twelve points every day, not only at meetings.
- Help other people at all times: look for chances to help without being asked.
- Physically strong: eat well, stay active, get enough sleep, and avoid harmful habits.
- Mentally awake: keep learning and stay curious.
- Morally straight: be honest, fair, and do what is right even when no one is watching.
> Practice saying it with the Scout sign at the start of every troop meeting.

# scout-law | Scout Law | The twelve points and what they mean
A Scout is trustworthy, loyal, helpful, friendly, courteous, kind, obedient, cheerful, thrifty, brave, clean, and reverent.
1. Trustworthy: tell the truth and keep your promises. People can depend on you.
2. Loyal: stand by your family, friends, patrol, troop, and country.
3. Helpful: do things willingly for others without expecting a reward.
4. Friendly: be a friend to everyone, including people who are different from you.
5. Courteous: be polite to everyone. Good manners make it easier for people to get along.
6. Kind: treat people and animals the way you want to be treated. Never harm living things without reason.
7. Obedient: follow the rules of your family, school, and troop. If a rule seems unfair, work to change it the right way.
8. Cheerful: look for the bright side. Do your tasks with a good attitude.
9. Thrifty: work to pay your own way, save for the future, and take care of property and natural resources.
10. Brave: face danger even when you are afraid. Stand up for what is right even if others laugh.
11. Clean: keep your body, mind, and surroundings clean. Help keep your community clean too.
12. Reverent: be faithful in your religious duties and respect the beliefs of others.
> Memory trick: say it in groups of three. Trustworthy-loyal-helpful, friendly-courteous-kind, obedient-cheerful-thrifty, brave-clean-reverent.

# motto-slogan | Motto and Slogan | Be Prepared. Do a Good Turn Daily.
## Scout Motto: Be Prepared
Be ready in mind and body for whatever might happen. That means learning first aid, packing the right gear, checking the weather, and thinking ahead about what could go wrong.
## Scout Slogan: Do a Good Turn Daily
Do something helpful for someone every day, without expecting anything in return. A Good Turn can be small: holding a door, helping a younger Scout with a knot, or picking up litter.
> At the end of each day ask yourself: what was my Good Turn today?

# outdoor-code | Outdoor Code | How a Scout treats the outdoors
As an American, I will do my best to:
- Be clean in my outdoor manners.
- Be careful with fire.
- Be considerate in the outdoors.
- Be conservation-minded.
## What it means
- Clean manners: leave a place cleaner than you found it. Pack out all trash.
- Careful with fire: use fire only where it is allowed, keep it small, and never leave it unattended.
- Considerate: respect other visitors, landowners, and wildlife. Keep noise down.
- Conservation-minded: protect soil, water, plants, and animals for the future.
See also the Leave No Trace section.

# sign-salute | Sign, Salute and Handclasp | Scout signals and greetings
## Scout sign
Raise your right arm, elbow bent, upper arm level with your shoulder. Hold up three fingers with your thumb covering your little finger. The three fingers stand for the three parts of the Scout Oath. Use it when saying the Oath or Law. When a leader raises the Scout sign, everyone gets quiet and raises theirs too.
## Scout salute
Make the Scout sign, then bring your index finger to the brim of your hat or to your right eyebrow. Salute the U.S. flag and to show respect to others.
## Scout handclasp
Shake hands with your left hand, the hand closer to your heart. It is a sign of friendship among Scouts.

# ranks | Ranks | The trail from Scout to Eagle
Scouts advance by learning skills, being active in the troop, showing Scout spirit, and meeting with leaders.
1. Scout: learn the Oath, Law, motto, slogan, sign, salute, handclasp, and basic knots and safety.
2. Tenderfoot: first camping trip, cooking, flag basics, first aid basics, physical fitness.
3. Second Class: navigation, fire building, more first aid, swimming skills, service.
4. First Class: orienteering, planning meals, advanced first aid, lashings, nature study.
5. Star: earn merit badges, hold a leadership position, and do service hours.
6. Life: more merit badges, leadership, service, and teaching younger Scouts.
7. Eagle: Scouting's highest rank. Earn the required merit badges, lead a service project that helps your community, and complete a board of review.
## How advancement works
- Learn the skill. Be tested by a qualified leader. Get it signed off. Be recognized.
- Each rank finishes with a Scoutmaster conference and a board of review.
- Merit badges each have a counselor who works with you.
! Requirements change. Always check your current Scouts BSA Handbook or ask a leader for the exact list.

# patrol-method | Patrol Method | How a troop runs itself
A patrol is a small team of about six to eight Scouts who camp, cook, and work together. The troop is run by its Scouts, led by the Senior Patrol Leader, with adult leaders as coaches.
## Common positions
- Senior Patrol Leader (SPL): leads the troop and runs meetings.
- Assistant Senior Patrol Leader (ASPL): helps the SPL and leads when the SPL is away.
- Patrol Leader: leads a patrol and represents it at the Patrol Leaders' Council.
- Quartermaster: keeps track of troop gear.
- Scribe: takes notes and keeps records.
- Troop Guide: helps new Scouts learn the ropes.
- Den Chief, Historian, Librarian, Chaplain Aide, Instructor, Outdoor Ethics Guide: other ways to lead and serve.
## Patrol identity
Pick a patrol name, flag, and yell. Patrols that have pride work better together.

# edge-method | Teaching with EDGE | How to teach any skill
Use EDGE when you teach a younger Scout a knot, first aid skill, or anything else.
1. Explain: tell them how it is done.
2. Demonstrate: show them, slowly, step by step.
3. Guide: let them try while you coach and correct.
4. Enable: let them do it on their own until they succeed, and help them become the next teacher.

# buddy-system | Buddy System | Never go alone
Scouts always pair up with a buddy for hikes, swimming, and outings. Buddies keep track of each other, help in an emergency, and make sure no one gets left behind.
- Know where your buddy is at all times.
- When a leader calls "buddy check," grab your buddy's hand and raise it.
- If you or your buddy gets hurt, one stays and one goes for help, but only if it is safe. In a group of three, two go for help together.
> A buddy is also someone to share the fun with. Pick a different buddy sometimes and make a new friend.
