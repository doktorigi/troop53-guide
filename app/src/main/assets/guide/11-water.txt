=== water | Water Safety | 🛶 | #1E6F8C | Swimming, boating and water rescue

# safe-swim | Safe Swim Defense | The eight points
Every Scout swim follows these eight points.
1. Qualified supervision: a trained, responsible adult supervises.
2. Personal health review: everyone's health is known before swimming.
3. Safe area: the swim area is checked and marked for each ability group.
4. Response personnel (lifeguards): trained rescuers stand ready with rescue equipment.
5. Lookout: someone watches the whole area from above.
6. Ability groups: swimmers, beginners, and non-swimmers stay in their own areas.
7. Buddy system: everyone swims with a buddy and checks in when called.
8. Discipline: everyone knows and follows the rules.
> Your swimming ability is tested each year so you can swim in the right area.

# safety-afloat | Safety Afloat | Boating rules
1. Qualified supervision
2. Personal health review
3. Swimming ability: only swimmers can go in most boats unless with a qualified adult.
4. Life jackets: everyone wears a properly fitted, buckled life jacket at all times.
5. Buddy system: every boat has a buddy boat.
6. Skill proficiency: everyone is trained in the boat they use.
7. Planning: know the route, the weather, and have a float plan.
8. Equipment: boats and gear are safe and right for the trip.
9. Discipline: everyone follows the rules.

# water-rescue | Water Rescue | Reach, throw, row, don't go
Never swim out to save someone unless you are trained in lifesaving. A panicking swimmer can pull you under.
1. Reach: lie down on the dock or shore and reach with your arm, a pole, a paddle, or a towel.
2. Throw: throw something that floats, like a ring buoy, life jacket, or cooler, ideally tied to a rope.
3. Row: use a boat to get to them, and have them hold the stern while you row to shore.
4. Go: only trained lifeguards should swim to a victim, with rescue equipment.
! Shout for help and send someone to call 911 while you help.

# life-jackets | Life Jackets | Fit it right
- Use a U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket sized for your weight.
- Buckle and zip everything.
- Fit test: have a buddy lift up on the shoulders. If it rises past your chin or ears, it is too loose.
- Wear it the whole time you are on the water. You cannot put it on after you fall in.

# cold-water | Cold Water | Cold shock is the hidden danger
- Falling into cold water causes a gasp and fast breathing for about a minute. Keep your face out of the water and focus on breathing.
- You have about 10 minutes of useful movement to get out or get to safety before your muscles get weak.
- If you cannot get out, keep your life jacket on, pull your knees to your chest, and wait for help.
- Treat for hypothermia once out of the water.
