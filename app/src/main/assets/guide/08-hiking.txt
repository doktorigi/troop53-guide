=== hiking | Hiking and Backpacking | 🥾 | #5C6B2E | Trail safety, layering, feet and water

# trail-safety | Trail Safety | Rules for every hike
- File a trip plan: where you are going, the route, and when you will be back.
- Stay with your group. The slowest hiker sets the pace.
- Stay on the trail. Do not shortcut switchbacks.
- Hike on the left side of roads, facing traffic, single file.
- Uphill hikers have the right of way. Step aside for horses on the downhill side.
- Take a break every hour. Drink and eat a snack.
- Turn around if the weather turns bad or someone is struggling. The summit will still be there.

# layering | Dressing in Layers | Stay COLD
Remember COLD:
- C: Keep clothes Clean. Dirt and oil reduce insulation.
- O: Avoid Overheating. Take off a layer before you sweat.
- L: Wear clothes Loose and in Layers.
- D: Stay Dry. Wet clothes steal body heat.
## Three layers
1. Base layer: wicks sweat away from skin. Synthetic or wool, never cotton.
2. Insulating layer: fleece, wool, or puffy jacket.
3. Shell: wind and rain jacket and pants.
! Cotton kills: when cotton gets wet it stops insulating and keeps you cold.

# foot-care | Footwear and Foot Care | Happy feet go farther
- Break in new boots before a big hike.
- Wear synthetic or wool socks, with a thin liner if you blister easily.
- Trim toenails short and straight across before the trip.
- Stop at the first hot spot and treat it.
- At breaks, take off boots and air out your feet.
- Change into dry socks when your socks get wet.

# packing-backpack | Packing a Backpack | Load it right
- Heavy items in the middle, close to your back, between shoulder blades.
- Sleeping bag at the bottom.
- Things you need during the day on top or in outside pockets: rain gear, first aid kit, water, snacks, map.
- Pack weight should be no more than about 25 percent of your body weight.
- Adjust the hip belt so it sits on your hip bones and carries most of the weight.
> Line your pack with a trash compactor bag to keep everything dry.

# water-treatment | Treating Water | Make water safe to drink
All water from lakes, streams, and springs can carry germs. Always treat it.
- Boil: bring to a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 feet).
- Filter: use a backpacking filter rated for bacteria and protozoa.
- Chemical: use purification tablets or drops. Follow the wait time on the label.
- UV light: a UV pen works on clear water.
> Collect from flowing water if possible, and let cloudy water settle or pre-filter it through a cloth first.

# pace-rest | Hiking Pace and Rest | Go steady, not fast
- Use the rest step on steep climbs: pause briefly with your back leg straight between each step.
- You should be able to talk while hiking. If you can't, slow down.
- Short breaks of 5 minutes keep muscles warm. Long breaks let you cool off and stiffen.
- Snack every hour: trail mix, granola bars, dried fruit.
- On average, plan about 2 miles per hour plus 1 hour for every 1,000 feet of climbing.
